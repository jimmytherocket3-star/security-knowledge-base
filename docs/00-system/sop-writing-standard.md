# Skills Hunter — SOP Writing Standard

## 1. Status and Use

This document is an approved-for-testing **Draft standard**. It is intended for use in future SOP drafting trials. It is not yet the final SOP standard and does not make any incident-specific procedure Approved.

## 2. Core Principle

A professional SOP must make clear why the procedure exists, when it starts, who is responsible, who has authority, what actions are required, how decisions and escalation work, what evidence is required, and when the work is complete.

**SOP SHALL NOT INVENT MISSING KNOWLEDGE.**

If Skills Hunter does not establish a required fact, authority, decision criterion, communication rule, or closure condition, the SOP draft must preserve it as a Knowledge Gap rather than infer or invent it.

If sources conflict, use the Skills Hunter conflict-management process. Do not silently select a winner.

## 4. Procedure Writing Model

Use the following working model when relevant:

**WHEN → WHO → DO → WHAT → HOW → IF → REPORT → RECORD → UNTIL**

Not every step must contain every element. Each operational step should identify a responsible role and a clear action. Information affecting life safety, decision authority, escalation, handover, or termination must not be left ambiguous when the source establishes it.

Prefer one primary action per step. Where actions are genuinely concurrent, state that explicitly rather than creating a false sequence.

## 5. Decision Points

Decision logic should identify, when applicable:

- Condition
- Criteria
- Decision authority
- Resulting action
- Alternative / exception path

Working form:

**IF [condition] → THEN [action] → BY [authority]**

If the criteria or authority are not established by Skills Hunter, record a Knowledge Gap.

## 6. Roles and Authority

Do not treat responsibility and authority as interchangeable.

- **Responsibility:** who performs the action.
- **Authority:** who may decide, declare, approve, escalate, terminate, or close.
- Where useful, distinguish **Primary**, **Support**, and **Informed** roles.

Avoid vague wording such as “coordinate with relevant units” when the source establishes the specific responsible parties.

## 7. Communication and Escalation

Distinguish:

- **Notification:** communication for awareness or required information.
- **Escalation:** elevation of response, decision authority, severity, or resources.

Where established, state trigger, sender, recipient, channel, required action, and authority.

## 8. Resources

Identify required people, systems, equipment, information, forms, references, or other resources when they are necessary to perform the procedure correctly.

## 9. Records and Evidence

Specify required operational records and evidence when established by source knowledge, such as case records, logs, reports, CCTV references, photographs, forms, or approvals.

Do not invent mandatory evidence requirements.

## 10. Termination, Handover, and Closure

Treat these as distinct concepts when applicable:

**Incident Controlled → Operational Termination → Handover / Return → Documentation Complete → Case Closure**

The end of an incident does not automatically mean that a case may be closed. Termination criteria, termination authority, closure requirements, and closure authority must come from supported knowledge.

## 11. Success Criteria

Define what constitutes successful completion of the procedure when the source establishes measurable or observable completion criteria.

## 12. Training and Presentation

SOP knowledge may be supported by text, images, flowcharts, checklists, video, or other appropriate training formats. Presentation format does not override the controlled written source.

## 13. SOP Quality Gate

Before an SOP is proposed for approval, review at least:

- Trigger
- Responsible Role
- Action
- Decision Criteria
- Decision Authority
- Escalation
- Communication
- Resources
- Safety / Restrictions
- Records / Evidence
- Termination
- Closure
- Success Criteria
- Source Traceability

Critical unresolved gaps involving trigger, life-safety decisions, authority, or termination/closure authority must remain visible and should block promotion to Approved status until resolved.

## 14. Knowledge-to-SOP Lifecycle

**Skills Hunter Knowledge → Source Validation → SOP Draft → Gap Detection → Gap Closure → Operational Review → Scenario / Exercise Test → KB Owner Approval → Approved SOP → Training → Incident / Exercise Findings → Review / Revision**

SOP drafting is also a KB quality-control mechanism: inability to write an operationally complete step may expose missing knowledge that requires gap closure.

## 15. Source Boundary

External standards and professional guidance may inform document structure, quality principles, emergency-planning concepts, and writing methodology.

They must not be used to invent One Bangkok internal facts, roles, authorities, routes, contacts, thresholds, or operational requirements. Those facts require applicable Skills Hunter sources.

## 16. Research Basis for v0.1

The working standard was synthesized for testing from:

- User-provided SOP learning material emphasizing clear purpose, required resources, success criteria, and multi-format learning support.
- ISO 10013:2021 concepts for documented information.
- OSHA emergency action planning concepts.
- FEMA CPG 101 emergency planning and coordination concepts.
- ASQ quality/SOP guidance.

These external sources are methodological references, not substitutes for Skills Hunter operational knowledge.

## 17. Pilot Validation

The first proposed validation exercise is to draft a **Code 1 SOP** from Skills Hunter knowledge.

The pilot should test:

- whether the structure is usable;
- whether operational steps are unambiguous;
- whether source traceability works;
- whether the Quality Gate exposes real Knowledge Gaps;
- whether the standard needs revision before promotion from v0.1 Draft.

No Code 1 SOP is approved merely by approval of this testing standard.
